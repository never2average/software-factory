# The desk briefing: the editors' rulebook

The rules the desk editors gave for the briefing, kept as they said them. `instructions.md` restates them; where the
two disagree, this file wins and the instructions are the defect.

## What a briefing is

1. One briefing per desk per edition day. It is written before the morning conference (the starter app refreshes it
   at 06:00 UTC on weekdays) and again on request.
2. Five sections, always in this order, each present even when empty ("Nothing."): **Due today**, **Embargoed**,
   **Unassigned**, **At risk**, **Published since the last briefing**.
3. Every line names the story's slug, its working title, who has it (or "nobody yet"), the time that matters for that
   section, and where the line came from in brackets.

## Which source wins

4. The assignments in the calendar (the records the newsroom keeps in the app) win over every export. A planning-sheet
   CSV, a calendar feed or the wire budget only ADD stories the calendar does not have yet; when they disagree with the
   calendar on a deadline or a reporter, the calendar's value is used and the disagreement is listed under At risk.
5. The wire budget is never a commitment: a wire story enters the briefing only as Unassigned until a desk editor
   assigns it.

## Times

6. Every time is written with its zone. A time that came without one is read in the desk's zone (UTC unless the desk
   says otherwise) and marked "(zone assumed)".
7. A deadline given as a day with no time is due at the edition cut-off, 17:00 in the desk's zone.
8. "Due now" means within 60 minutes. A deadline in the past for a story not yet filed puts it under At risk.

## Embargoes

9. A story under an embargo that has not lifted is listed under Embargoed with the time it lifts, never under Due
   today, whatever its deadline.
10. An embargo with no time is not an embargo the briefing can honour: list the story under At risk, "embargo time
    missing".

## At risk

11. At risk: past its deadline and not filed; due within two hours with no reporter; a reporter with three or more
    stories due the same day; a calendar/export disagreement (rule 4); an embargo with no time (rule 10). Each says
    why in words.

## What to leave out

12. Spiked stories, and published stories older than the last briefing.
13. Nothing about a person beyond the name or address the calendar gives (no availability guesses).

## Open points

- **Several desks in one export.** The editors have not said whether a combined planning sheet should produce one
  briefing per desk or one combined briefing. Meanwhile: one briefing per desk, and the export's other desks are
  ignored with a line saying how many rows were skipped.
- **Weekend editions.** Not yet defined; meanwhile the Friday briefing covers Saturday and Sunday under Due today,
  each line with its day.
